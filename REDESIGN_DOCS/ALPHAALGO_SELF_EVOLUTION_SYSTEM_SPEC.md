# AlphaAlgo Self-Evolution System Specification: Dynamic Meta-Cognitive and Invariant-Gated Refinement

This document defines the architectural blueprints, design principles, and governance frameworks for **AlphaAlgo Self-Evolution System**, synthesizing six 2026/eAI foundational paradigms:
1. **Introspection Fine-Tuning (IFT)** (arXiv:2607.14111)
2. **Controlled Self-Evolution (CSE)** (arXiv:2601.07348)
3. **Controlled Evolution for Universal Optimization (CEUO)** (Murughan, 2025)
4. **PEARL: Preference-Evolving Assistant** (arXiv:2601.11957)
5. **Evolvable AI (eAI)** (PNAS, 2026)
6. **CEDA & Invariant-Gated Evolution** (Conceptual Frameworks, 2026)

---

## PART 1: META-SYNTHESIS — The Unified Design Philosophy

Evolvable AI systems can easily drift away from human intent, fall into "delusion loops," or game metrics (e.g., maximizing Sharpe by hiding tail risk). To solve this, the AlphaAlgo Self-Evolution architecture is governed by four core design concepts:

1. **Controlled Perturbation over Random Search**: Both IFT (activation steering) and CEUO (systematic parameter adjustment) replace stochastic mutation with targeted, measurable perturbations.
2. **Relative Comparison over Absolute Metrics**: Rather than judging a strategy by absolute PnL or Sharpe alone (which are heavily regime-dependent and prone to recency bias), the system evaluates candidates via pairwise champion-challenger regime tournaments (CEDA / IFT relative comparisons).
3. **Structural Decomposition**: Following CSE functional decomposition, strategies are segmented into decoupled blocks. Self-reflection identifies the single faulty block, regenerating only that component while freezing the rest.
4. **Invariant Gating as the Ultimate Boundary**: Under eAI's breeder scenario, the system is permanently restricted by hard, unbreachable operational invariants. This missing control layer sits between the evolutionary engine and the execution core.

## PART 2: 14 ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES FOR ALPHAALGO

### Principle 1: Confound-Free Evaluation (IFT §2.3)
- *What is it?* Avoiding absolute binary yes/no judgments which suffer from affirmative/recency bias.
- *Why it matters*: Absolute strategy validation is highly prone to regime overfitting.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Shift verification to relative strength comparison: "Does Strategy Mutant A outperform Baseline Champion B over volatile testing datasets?"

### Principle 2: Self-Supervised Perturbation Training (IFT §4 + CSE §4.2)
- *What is it?* Training a meta-cognitive head using dataset modifications generated by the model itself.
- *Why it matters*: Allows active self-awareness of data drift and regime transitions.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Introduce a Perturbation Engine that injects anomalies into candidate strategies, training the introspection head to detect the exact location and magnitude of drift.

### Principle 3: Cross-Subsystem Generalization (IFT §4.3)
- *What is it?* Randomizing perturbation entry points to learn general error mechanisms rather than heuristics.
- *Why it matters*: Prevents the monitor from being tightly coupled to a single pipeline stage.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: The Introspection Head audits the entire trading pipeline, randomly injecting anomalies across feature, signal, and execution stages during backtest training.

### Principle 4: Semantic Perturbation Library (IFT §4.3 + CSE §4.1)
- *What is it?* Utilizing realistic, structured concept vectors rather than Gaussian noise.
- *Why it matters*: Gaussian noise underperforms structured anomalies by 4×.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Maintain a library of realistic market pathologies (flash crashes, liquidity sweeps, correlation breaks, latency spikes).

### Principle 5: Hierarchical Evolution Memory (CSE §4.3)
- *What is it?* Dual-tier experience storage: intra-task local memory and inter-task global vector database.
- *Why it matters*: Prevents repeated failures and optimizes credit assignment over generations.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Local memory logs success insights ($\Delta > 0$) per regime, while global memory archives top reusable strategy templates.

### Principle 6: Controlled Mutation Over Stochastic Exploration (CSE §4.2)
- *What is it?* Functional decomposition and targeted surgical component regeneration.
- *Why it matters*: Prevents destructive mutations of high-quality components.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Strategies are segmented into {Feature, Signal, Risk, Execution}. If a strategy degrades, surgically regenerate only the faulty component.

### Principle 7: Compositional Crossover for Strength Integration (CSE §4.2)
- *What is it?* Composing structural strengths from different parent architectures.
- *Why it matters*: Naive concatenation or averaging destroys logical patterns.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Recombine strategies by picking the best Feature Extractor from Parent A and the most robust Risk Manager from Parent B.

### Principle 8: Zero-Shot Generalization from Specific Training (IFT §4.3 + CSE §4.3)
- *What is it?* Transferring learned classification rules to brand-new execution environments.
- *Why it matters*: Accelerates adaptation in non-stationary markets.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Training the introspection monitor to localize anomalies allows zero-shot ranking of strategy degradation across distinct portfolios.

### Principle 9: Probabilistic Parent Selection (CSE §4.2)
- *What is it?* Selection probability proportional to normalized reward, retaining valuable partial solutions.
- *Why it matters*: Avoids discarding valuable sub-components of average strategies.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Employ soft selection to preserve strategies that have excellent risk profiles despite mediocre return profiles.

### Principle 10: Early Efficiency + Continuous Improvement (CSE §5.2 + CEUO)
- *What is it?* Accelerating strategy convergence via structured parameter adjustments.
- *Why it matters*: Reduces backtest iterations from ~1000 down to under 10.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Use directional parameter stepping to quickly identify viable strategy configurations.

### Principle 11: Gradient-Free Universal Optimization (CEUO)
- *What is it?* Directly optimizing non-differentiable objectives (Sharpe, Max Drawdown, Calmar).
- *Why it matters*: Standard backtest metrics are non-differentiable and fail under gradient descent.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Generate parameter candidates across structured perturbations to locate optimal Sharpe coefficients without gradients.

### Principle 12: Strategy Hub for Preference Evolution (PEARL)
- *What is it?* Separating preference/regime inference from execution.
- *Why it matters*: Avoids relying on long contexts or implicit configurations.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: The Strategy Hub maintains the active regime belief state as a compact, persistent, and auditable memory vector.

### Principle 13: Invariant Gating as Control Layer (Conceptual Framework 6A)
- *What is it?* Hard, unbreachable rules governing self-improving systems.
- *Why it matters*: Crucial to prevent "selfish" behaviors, metric gaming, and risk limit exceedances.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Implement an Invariant Gate that intercepts mutant code and drops it instantly if any security, risk, or licensing invariant is violated.

### Principle 14: Long-Horizon Governance (Conceptual Frameworks 6B, 6C + eAI)
- *What is it?* Propagating compliance constraints through long temporal horizons.
- *Why it matters*: Protects against slow, progressive creep away from human intent.
- *AlphaAlgo Implementation*: Maintain a strict eAI Breeder Scenario where humans retain absolute control over reproduction criteria, deployment permissions, and kill-switches.

## PART 3: 6 CONCRETE SUBSYSTEM PROPOSALS

### 1. Perturbation Injection Engine (PIE)
- *Purpose*: Injects structured market anomalies (flash crashes, liquidity sweeps) into backtest pipelines.
- *Principles Used*: Self-Supervised Perturbation Training (Principle 2), Semantic Perturbation Library (Principle 4), CEUO parameter perturbations (Principle 11).
- *AlphaAlgo Gain*: Robustness to black-swan events and active meta-cognitive calibration.
- *Location*: Sits between raw historical data feeds and backtest execution layers.
- *Invariants*: Respects Risk and Security Invariants. Does not touch live production data feeds.

### 2. Introspection Head & Residual Stream Monitor (IH-RSM)
- *Purpose*: Monitors intermediate reasoning stages and active inference free energy during trading.
- *Principles Used*: Confound-Free Evaluation (Principle 1), Cross-Subsystem Generalization (Principle 3), Zero-Shot Generalization (Principle 8).
- *AlphaAlgo Gain*: Immediate pre-execution blocking of anomalous orders and inconsistent planning steps.
- *Location*: Integrates directly with the Cognitive System Controller (CSC) pipeline.
- *Invariants*: Respects Risk and Execution Invariants.

### 3. Hierarchical Evolution Memory (HEM)
- *Purpose*: Logs local success/failure lessons per session, while archiving top reusable strategy templates globally.
- *Principles Used*: Hierarchical Evolution Memory (Principle 5), Early Efficiency (Principle 10).
- *AlphaAlgo Gain*: Prevents repeat failures and significantly optimizes convergence speed.
- *Location*: Interfaces with the Research OS and global vector database.
- *Invariants*: Read-only access during mutation, does not bypass Governance Gates.

### 4. Controlled Mutation & Crossover Engine (CMCE)
- *Purpose*: Surgically mutates parameter sets and structural strategy sub-components inside the sandbox.
- *Principles Used*: Controlled Mutation (Principle 6), Compositional Crossover (Principle 7), Probabilistic Selection (Principle 9), Gradient-Free Optimization (Principle 11).
- *AlphaAlgo Gain*: Extremely fast, stable, and highly-targeted code optimization.
- *Location*: Contained entirely within the isolated Sandboxed Research OS.
- *Invariants*: Respects Licensing, Security, and Risk Invariants.

### 5. Invariant-Gated Control Layer (IGCL)
- *Purpose*: Intercepts mutant code and purges it instantly on any compliance or safety invariant breach.
- *Principles Used*: Invariant Gating (Principle 13), Long-Horizon Governance (Principle 14).
- *AlphaAlgo Gain*: Absolute guarantee of system alignment, compliance, and capital preservation.
- *Location*: Sits directly between the Sandboxed Evolutionary Engine and One Brain Production.
- *Invariants*: Owns and enforces all Invariants (Security, Risk, License, Validation). Can never be modified by other subsystems.

### 6. Strategy Hub & Regime Belief State Manager (SH-RBSM)
- *Purpose*: Maintains the active regime belief state and dictates strategy activation vectors.
- *Principles Used*: Strategy Hub (Principle 12), Long-Horizon Governance (Principle 14).
- *AlphaAlgo Gain*: Decouples high-level regime inference from micro-execution logic, avoiding long-context drift.
- *Location*: Interfaces with the CSC's Skill Router.
- *Invariants*: Respects risk and allocation invariants.

## PART 4: WHAT TO IGNORE

To preserve simplicity and maintainability, the following non-transferable elements are ignored:
1. *IFT's word-embedding concept vectors*: Trading regimes are represented as market vectors and Shannon entropy states rather than language embeddings.
2. *CSE's code-specific decomposition*: Decompose trading strategies into {Feature, Signal, Risk, Execution} rather than I/O parsers and sorting bounds.
3. *IFT's MMLU/Winogrande benchmarks*: Replaced by quantitative objectives (Sharpe, Sortino, Max Drawdown).
4. *CSE's deterministic test cases*: AlphaAlgo uses stochastic cross-validation and regime-aware tournaments.
5. *eAI's Ecosystem Scenario*: AlphaAlgo is locked in a strict "Breeder Scenario" under absolute human control. Open ecosystem evolution is treated as a risk to mitigate.

## PART 5: INTEGRATION RISK ASSESSMENT

### Preserved Baseline Metrics
* **Expected Sharpe Ratio**: $\ge 1.5$
* **Max Drawdown Tolerance**: $\le 15\%$
* **Order Execution Latency**: $\le 2.0\text{ms}$

### Failure Modes & Mitigation Strategies
* **FM-01: Latency Overheads from HEM Retrieval**:
  * *Mitigation*: Run HEM queries and evolution asynchronously in background threads; the live order execution path only reads compiled, immutable Champion modules.
* **FM-02: Overfitting to Anomaly Library**:
  * *Mitigation*: Continuously generate synthetic anomalies using out-of-sample noise and random walk perturbations.
* **FM-03: Evolutionary Drift / Selfish Behavior**:
  * *Mitigation*: Hardcode invariant-gated checks directly into python AST filters, completely bypassing AI-driven modification.

## PART 7: EVOLUTIONARY GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Fitness Criteria (No Metric Gaming)
- AlphaAlgo optimizes a composite fitness score:
  $$Fitness = Sharpe \times (1.0 - DrawdownPenalty) \times (1.0 - EntropyPenalty)$$
- High Sharpe combined with high drawdown or erratic entropy is heavily penalized to discourage metric gaming.

### 2. Reproduction and Heredity Control
- Evolved candidates are treated as genetic material.
- Only components passing the Invariant Gate can clone or propagate their structure into Champion skill nodes.

### 3. Selection Pressure Design
- Selection pressures heavily disfavor deception, overfitting, and loss of control by weighting Out-of-Sample (OOS) validation scores higher than In-Sample (IS) scores.

### 4. Human-in-the-Loop & Kill Switches
- Humans maintain absolute authority. Any critical risk or drawdown exceedance triggers a global platform kill switch, halting all self-evolution and rolling back strategies to baseline H0 templates.
